# Planning partners and ezCater pricing

The landing page now prioritizes event planning and reimbursement preparation. The public group directory, event listing, and guidance cards were removed from the page because RevConnect already offers browsing; planning questions still retrieve published records. The event budget tool identifies ezCater, Instacart, Amazon, and Uber for Business as Org Help purchasing partners in one restrained logo strip without vendor hyperlinks. The food workspace recommends ezCater first and records the purchasing channel, restaurant, service style, headcount, delivery date/time/location/ZIP, dietary needs, food budget cap, menu subtotal, delivery, service, tip, and quoted tax. It compares the entered quote's food total with the cap, creates a copyable staff handoff brief, and can transfer the event total and headcount into the funding draft. For ezCater group ordering, it computes a planning allowance per attendee after entered fees; the actual per-person cap must still be confirmed in ezCater. A no-food plan hides and ignores the food fields. The page neither links to vendors nor places orders; every result labels entered quotes and assumptions as non-live.

Generic planning questions prioritize [GW's organization finance guidance](https://students.gwu.edu/organization-finances) and [Org Help support guidance](https://students.gwu.edu/org-advising-support). An explicit group or event question can still retrieve those public listings, but generic planning answers no longer surface unrelated group names as finance guidance.

Logo asset provenance: the current ezCater mark is from [ezCater's own website](https://www.ezcater.com/), the Instacart SVG comes from its [official logo pack](https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/concepts/design/logos), the Amazon image is from its [press center](https://press.aboutamazon.com/logos), and the Uber for Business SVG is served by [Uber's official business site](https://www.uber.com/us/en/business/). These marks remain the property of their respective owners and are used here only to identify the purchasing partners.

The publicly documented [ezCater developer API](https://api.ezcater.io/) is aimed at restaurant partners integrating menus, order events, and fulfillment. Its [Orders API](https://api.ezcater.io/orders-api) covers order notifications and restaurant acceptance/rejection, not an anonymous buyer quote service. The available documentation does not establish a buyer endpoint for searching the marketplace or calculating a live checkout quote for an arbitrary event. ezCater describes [group ordering](https://www.ezcater.com/lunchrush/office/group-ordering-on-ezcater/) with a per-person budget and guest selection, which informs the planning allowance shown here. The published [procurement PunchOut integration](https://help.ezcater.io/en/articles/16866803-procurement-punchout) requires an enterprise procurement setup. ezCater also documents an [admin-controlled Concur integration](https://help.ezcater.io/en/articles/11592223-what-integrations-are-available-with-ezcater-enterprise), but the organization would have to enable it and it is separate from live buyer pricing. No ezCater API credentials or buyer integration were provided for this project.

Therefore, the site must not scrape undocumented endpoints or present simulated prices as live ezCater quotes. To add live pricing later, obtain an authorized buyer-side interface from the organization's ezCater enterprise representative, including endpoint documentation, scope, rate limits, permitted data use, and pricing terms. Only then should a server-side adapter with a secret credential be built, with an explicit unavailable state when no quote can be returned. That adapter must remain within the project's Cloudflare free-plan limits and must not receive reimbursement files.
